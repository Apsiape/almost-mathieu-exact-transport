\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The dynamical picture.}
For comparison with the upper exponents, define
\[
\beta^-(p)=\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{\log M_p(T)}{p\log T},
\qquad \alpha_u^-=\sup_{p>0}\beta^-(p).
\]
Damanik and Tcheremchantsev proved that these lower Abel-averaged transport exponents vanish for every $\lambda>1$, every irrational frequency and every phase~\cite[Theorem~4]{DT2007}. For $0<\log\lambda<\beta(\alpha)<\infty$ and $\theta\in\Ga^*$, Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives $\beta^+(p)=\au=1-\log\lambda/\beta(\alpha)$ for every $p>0$. When $\beta(\alpha)\le\log\lambda$, the upper exponents also vanish at phases in $\Ga^*$. The positive-exponent regime is therefore intermittent: upper exponents record resonant bursts, whereas lower exponents record sequences of confinement times. More precisely, fix $\zeta>0$ and $c>\log\lambda+\zeta$. For $\theta\in\Ga^*$ and $\beta(\alpha)<\infty$, at sufficiently large scales with $b_n\ge c$, and with $T=|\delta_n|^{-1}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B} and Remark~\ref{rem:burst}, with $h=\log\lambda+\zeta$, put Abel-averaged mass $1-o(1)$ between $T\lambda^{-q_n}e^{-\zeta q_n}$ and $2T\lambda^{-q_n}e^{\zeta q_n}$, with exponentially small mass beyond the outer radius. Corollary~\ref{cor:almostalltimes} shows that, for each permitted averaging scale $T$ and fixed $b>0$ and $s\in(0,1)$, the instantaneous mass outside the corresponding annulus exceeds $s$ at only $o(T)$ times in $[0,bT]$. This does not determine the asymptotic density of burst times across all scales.

\paragraph{Universality.}
For $0<\beta(\alpha)<\infty$, Theorem~\ref{thm:L} gives the fastest-exponent lower bound at every phase for Lipschitz sampling functions whose Lyapunov exponent is continuous near a spectral minimizer. The rest of this paper uses the almost Mathieu structure: Chambers' formula makes the phase dependence of the rational discriminant a single cosine with energy-independent amplitude. The released companion~\cite[Theorem~A]{DMuniversality} proves the exact fastest-exponent law $\max\{0,1-\ell_*/\beta(\alpha)\}$ for almost every phase of every real-analytic operator that is supercritical and of type~I on its spectrum, where $\ell_*=\min_\Sigma L$. Its lower bound holds at every phase; at $\beta=0$ and $\beta=\infty$ its equality holds at every phase, with the same endpoint conventions as here. If $v(x)=w(kx)$, the operator has reduced sampling function $w$, frequency $k\alpha$ and phase $k\theta$, so the relevant arithmetic quantity is $\beta(k\alpha)$. Beyond type~I, the companion proves the almost-every-phase fastest-exponent law, with $\beta(s\alpha)$ in place of $\beta(\alpha)$, for $v(x)=w(sx)$ whose primitive part $w$ is supercritical and of type~I~\cite[Theorem~C]{DMuniversality}, and, for every real-analytic $v$ with positive Lyapunov exponent on the spectrum, conditionally on a separation hypothesis for the periodic approximants of the primitive sampling function~\cite[Theorem~D]{DMuniversality}.

\paragraph{Hausdorff versus packing dimension.} Jitomirskaya and Last proved that for $\lambda>1$ the spectral measures of the almost Mathieu operator have zero Hausdorff dimension~\cite{JL2000}. For $0<\log\lambda<\beta(\alpha)<\infty$ and $\theta\in\Ga^*$, Theorem~\ref{thm:C} gives packing dimension $1-\log\lambda/\beta(\alpha)>0$ for every nonzero spectral measure, whereas its Hausdorff dimension is zero. Thus these measures have different Hausdorff and packing dimensions at the same phases where lower transport exponents vanish and upper ones are positive. Theorem~\ref{thm:renyi} also determines the upper and lower generalized R\'enyi dimensions of the canonical spectral measure for orders between zero and one. Exact values at other orders, and analogous statements for arbitrary initial vectors with uncontrolled spatial tails, are not determined here.

\paragraph{Exceptional phases.} The upper bounds are proved for $\theta\in\Ga^*$, a full-measure set invariant under $\theta\mapsto\theta+\alpha$ that tolerates any subexponential decay of $|\sin(2\pi q_n\theta)|$ along scales with $b_n\ge c$. The transport and packing lower bounds of Theorems~\ref{thm:A}--\ref{thm:C} hold for every phase. Exceptional phases can nevertheless have larger fastest exponents, as the released companion proves~\cite[Theorems~B(1) and~C]{DMworstcase}. Let $\beta_e(\alpha)$ be the limsup of $b_n$ along even denominators $q_n$, with value zero if there are only finitely many. If $\beta_e(\alpha)=\beta(\alpha)\in[\log\lambda,\infty)$, then
\[
\sup_{\theta\in\T}\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,\theta})
=\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,1/4})
=1-\frac{\log\lambda}{2\beta(\alpha)}.
\]
The same value occurs on a dense $G_\delta$ set of phases and is strictly larger than the almost-every-phase value. This is a theorem about the fastest exponent, not equality of all moment exponents at the exceptional phases. Without any condition on even denominators, the same companion bounds every phase uniformly: for $\log\lambda\le\beta(\alpha)<\infty$, $\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,\theta})\le1-\log\lambda/(2\beta(\alpha))$ at every phase, and for $0<\beta(\alpha)<\log\lambda$ the corresponding bound is $\beta(\alpha)/(\beta(\alpha)+\log\lambda)$~\cite[Theorem~A and Corollary~5.6]{DMworstcase}.

\paragraph{Turning phases.} The barrier of Proposition~\ref{prop:local} holds at every phase. At an exact turning phase of the drifting cosine, where $y_0=2\cos(2\pi q_n\theta)=\pm2$, such as $\theta=0$ at every scale, the confinement comes from the quadratic part of the cosine: for $b_n>L$ the radius $e^{(b_n-L+\eps)q_n}$ becomes $e^{(b_n-L/2+\eps)q_n}$, and for $b_n<L$ the proof gives only $e^{(b_n/2+O(\eps))q_n}$. The reflection-symmetric phases $\theta=(m+k\alpha)/2$ with $k\ne0$ are not exact turning phases: since $|\sin(2\pi q_n\theta)|=|\sin(\pi k\delta_n)|$, the value $y_0$ only approaches $\pm2$, exponentially fast along the resonant scales, and this is what excludes them from $\Ga^*$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Gastar}). At turning phases the non-turning inverse-branch argument of Section~\ref{sec:filters} no longer supplies the upper velocity scale $\lambda^{-q}$. The filters are centred at $y_0$, and the sine-based lower bound~\eqref{eq:Pprime} degenerates when $y_0$ equals or is exponentially close to $\pm2$. A filter of width $\rho$ requires the relevant real inverse branches $e_b$ to extend smoothly across $[y_0-4\rho,y_0+4\rho]$, with $\rho\ge e^{-o(q)}$. Requiring every critical value of $P_q$ to have modulus greater than $(2+4\rho)\lambda^q$ is a sufficient stronger condition; the actual obstruction is a critical value in the required interval after division by $\lambda^q$. Whether the branches extend depends on the relevant gaps of the periodic approximant. At $\theta=0$ and scales with $4\mid q_n$ it fails. For even $q$ the conserved polynomial is even and satisfies $P_q(0)=2(-1)^{q/2}(\lambda^q+1)$~\cite[Corollary~7.2]{DMworstcase}; so for $4\mid q$ the critical value at $E=0$ is $(2+2\lambda^{-q})\lambda^q$, and the central gap of the approximant $H_{\lambda,p/q,0}$ is closed. Next to this closed gap, the companion's argument constructs, for every fixed $h>L$ and $0<\eta\le1/4$, an elliptic energy window of width at least $c_{\eta,h}q^{-1}e^{-hq/2}$. This gives the polynomial-tail lower bound $\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,0})\ge\max\{0,1-\log\lambda/(2\beta_4(\alpha))\}$ derived in Remark~\ref{rem:phasezero}, where $\beta_4(\alpha)$ is the limsup of $b_n$ along denominators divisible by four, and the example there has exponent $3/4$ at $\theta=0$ and $1/2$ at almost every phase. The exponent at a turning phase is therefore not determined by the radius of the quadratic barrier alone. We do not know it for other arithmetic patterns of the resonant denominators, where the gap at the turning point may stay open, and we do not know whether the packing dimension depends on the phase. The single-scale finite-volume observations in Remark~\ref{rem:tpnum} are consistent with the radius $\lambda^{-q/2}/|\delta|$, both for $b_n>\log\lambda$ and for $\frac12\log\lambda<b_n<\log\lambda$; in the latter examples the non-turning phases had much smaller observed radii. They concern one observable at small denominators and say nothing about exponents.

\begin{remark}[The phase $0$ along denominators divisible by four]\label{rem:phasezero}
This remark records a consequence of the argument of~\cite[Theorem~7.5]{DMworstcase}, applied at $\theta=0$ along the denominators divisible by four. It is not a result printed in~\cite{DMworstcase}, whose exponent conclusion is stated only at the phase $1/4$. Let $\lambda>1$, $L=\log\lambda$, let $\alpha$ be irrational, and put
\[
\beta_4(\alpha)=\limsup_{n:\ 4\mid q_n}b_n ,
\]
with $\beta_4(\alpha)=0$ if only finitely many $q_n$ are divisible by four. Then
\[
\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,0})\ge\max\Bigl\{0,\ 1-\frac{L}{2\beta_4(\alpha)}\Bigr\}.
\]
The right side is read as $0$ if $\beta_4(\alpha)=0$ and as $1$ if $\beta_4(\alpha)=\infty$. The argument has four steps.

(i) \emph{Closed gap.} For even $q$, \cite[Corollary~7.2]{DMworstcase} gives $P_q(0)=2(-1)^{q/2}(\lambda^q+1)$ and $P_q(-E)=P_q(E)$. If $4\mid q$, the discriminant $\Delta_t=P_q-2\lambda^q$ of the approximant $H_{\lambda,p/q,0}$ therefore satisfies $\Delta_t(0)=2$ and $\Delta_t'(0)=0$: its central gap is closed.

(ii) \emph{Hypotheses and phase condition.} \cite[Theorem~7.5]{DMworstcase} assumes $\lambda>1$, $\alpha$ irrational, $h>L$ and $\eta\in(0,1/4]$, and works at scales with $q_n$ even and $b_n\ge h/2+(h-L)$, at averaging times $e^{(h/2+(h-L))q_n}\le T\le|\delta_n|^{-1}$, and, besides the phase $1/4$, at every phase $\varphi$ with $|\cos(2\pi q_n\varphi)-(-1)^{q_n/2}|\le\eta\lambda^{-q_n}/16$. At $\varphi=0$ and $4\mid q_n$ the left side is $|1-1|=0$. For such a phase the argument compares the first $q_n$-block of $H_{\lambda,\alpha,\varphi}$ to the right of the origin with the rational block $A^{(p_n/q_n)}_{q_n}(E,\varphi+p_n/q_n)$; at $\varphi=0$ its trace is $\Delta_t$, which by~(i) has a closed gap at $E=0$, and the potentials of the two blocks differ by at most $4\pi\lambda|\delta_n|$ at each site.

(iii) \emph{Tail estimate.} There are then $c_\eta>0$ and $n_0$ such that, for every $n\ge n_0$ with $4\mid q_n$ and $b_n\ge h/2+(h-L)$, and every $T$ with $e^{(h/2+(h-L))q_n}\le T\le|\delta_n|^{-1}$,
\[
P_0\bigl(\lfloor c_\eta Te^{-hq_n/2}\rfloor,T\bigr)\ge T^{-2}e^{-2hq_n},
\]
where $P_0$ is the outside probability for $H_{\lambda,\alpha,0}$ and the initial state $\delta_0$. The ingredients are an energy $E^{(1)}$ next to the closed gap with $|\Delta_t(E^{(1)})|=2-\eta$ and $|P_{q_n}'(E^{(1)})|\le q_n\sqrt{2C_h\eta}\,e^{hq_n/2}$~\cite[Proposition~7.4]{DMworstcase}, the insertion lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:insert}) and an elliptic run of the Weyl solution (Lemma~\ref{lem:run}). The statement of~\cite[Theorem~7.5]{DMworstcase} includes this tail estimate, with modified constants, for every phase satisfying the condition in~(ii).

(iv) \emph{Exponent.} At $T_n=|\delta_n|^{-1}=e^{b_nq_n}$ the tail in~(iii) is at least $T_n^{-2-2h/b_n}$ beyond a radius at least $c_\eta T_n^{1-h/(2b_n)}-1$. Since $M_p(T)\ge N^pP(N,T)$, along scales with $4\mid q_n$ and $b_n\to\beta_4(\alpha)$ we get $\beta^+(p)\ge1-h/(2\beta_4(\alpha))-(2+2h/\beta_4(\alpha))/p$ whenever $h/2+(h-L)<\beta_4(\alpha)<\infty$. Let $p\to\infty$ and then $h\downarrow L$. If $\beta_4(\alpha)\le L/2$ there is nothing to prove, and if $\beta_4(\alpha)=\infty$ the same computation with $b_n\to\infty$ gives $1$.

If $\beta_4(\alpha)=\beta(\alpha)\in[L,\infty)$, the all-phase bound $\au\le1-L/(2\beta(\alpha))$ of~\cite[Theorem~A and Corollary~5.6]{DMworstcase} gives equality: $\au(H_{\lambda,\alpha,0})=1-L/(2\beta(\alpha))$. For example, take $\lambda=2$ and the computable frequency with partial quotients $a_1=4$, $a_{2k}=4^{q_{2k-1}}$ and $a_{2k+1}=4$ for $k\ge1$. Since $q_0=1$, $q_1=4$ and $4^{q}\equiv0\pmod4$ for $q\ge1$, induction gives $q_{2k-1}\equiv0$ and $q_{2k}\equiv1\pmod4$. Along $n=2k-1$ we have $q_n^{-1}\log q_{n+1}=\log4+O(q_n^{-1}\log q_n)$, and along $n=2k$ this ratio tends to $0$; by~\eqref{eq:bn} the same holds for $b_n$. Hence $\beta_4(\alpha)=\beta(\alpha)=2\log2=2L$, and $\au(H_{2,\alpha,0})=3/4$, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives $1/2$ at almost every phase.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The size of the fraction.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} gives the fraction $1-o(1)$. More precisely, for each fixed $0<\eta\le1/4$, throughout its permitted resonant windows,
\[
1-P(Te^{-hq_n},T)
\le 1-\frac2\pi\arcsin\Bigl(1-\frac{3\eta}2\Bigr)
 +C_\eta q_ne^{-(h-\log\lambda)q_n/2}+o_\eta(1),
\]
uniformly in the phase and in $T$. The last term comes from convergence in the arcsine law; no explicit rate for it is given here. First taking the scale limit and then $\eta\downarrow0$ makes the deficit vanish. The behavior well after the resonant time remains open: for $T\gg|q_n\alpha-p_n|^{-1}$ the elliptic propagation estimates are limited by the drift $|q_n\alpha-p_n|$ rather than the damping $1/T$, so the near-block count used here no longer gives a fraction tending to one. This does not affect the exponent proof, which uses resonant averaging times.

\paragraph{Critical coupling.} At $\lambda=1$ the Lyapunov exponent vanishes. For $\beta(\alpha)>0$, Jitomirskaya and Zhang proved quasiballistic transport at every phase~\cite[Corollary~3]{JZ2022}. For $\beta(\alpha)=0$ both halves of our method fail: the velocity $\lambda^{-q}$ of the upper bound becomes $1$, and the elliptic propagation estimates need scales with $|q_n\alpha-p_n|\le e^{-cq_n}$ for some $c>0$, which then do not exist. Transport at critical coupling with $\beta(\alpha)=0$ is a separate problem.

\paragraph{Physical meaning.}
The golden mean has $\beta(\alpha)=0$, and the upper power-law transport exponents vanish for $\lambda>1$ at every phase~\cite[Theorem~4]{DT2007}. Positive upper exponents can occur for other irrational frequencies at the same coupling. The finite-scale statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:B}: for fixed $c>h>\log\lambda$, along sufficiently large denominators with $b_n\ge c$, the Abel-averaged mass beyond $Te^{-hq_n}$ tends to one uniformly in phase and in $e^{hq_n}\le T\le|\delta_n|^{-1}$. Such scales require correspondingly large next partial quotients; the theorem does not assert this behavior for an arbitrary single large partial quotient or from time $\lambda^{q_n}$ without an exponential margin. The exponent is an infinite-time quantity of an infinite system. Its noncomputability for the explicit operator of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}(3) does not prevent effective approximation of finite-time probabilities for computable data and computable times. Figure~\ref{fig:burst} gives finite-volume, finite-time observations of radii comparable to $1/(|\delta|\lambda^q)$ and initial growth comparable to the reference speed $\lambda^{-q}$; it does not measure a limsup exponent.

\subsection*{How this work was done}

The authors developed the results and proofs with assistance from AI models from more than one developer. They take responsibility for every claim. The authors chose the question, directed the work and decided which lines to pursue; the models did most of the exploration, derivation and drafting.

The proofs of version 1.0.0 were developed over several long working sessions, each building on the previous ones and recording its proofs, failed routes and self-checks. Each step was then checked by a separate session that had not seen the material, with instructions to refute it and to read the primary sources it relies on, and the repairs found were incorporated. The mathematics new in this version was checked by one blind, refutation-first session that had not seen the paper; its findings, including two false statements that are now repaired, are incorporated here. The text then had an editorial review and a reconciliation of the references and citation locators. Priority and literature searches were AI-assisted. The proofs are analytic. Numerical computations appear only in remarks and figures labelled as illustrations, and are not used in any proof. The finite-volume estimate in Section~\ref{sec:finitetime} provides an explicit error bound for numerical evaluation; finite simulations are not used to infer the asymptotic transport law.
